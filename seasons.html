Where we are now

Winter · your period

Winter
Menstruation · your period
What is happening
  • Hormones at their lowest; the body sheds the lining.
  • For many, the most tired, low-energy week.
  • Cramps, heaviness or low mood are common and normal.
  • "Everything feels like a mountain."
Winter
Working with it
Social battery
How to work with it
  • School: a low-stakes week: review, organise, flashcards.
  • Movement: gentle activity can ease cramps. You can still train. Rest is allowed.
  • Social: running near empty. Saying no is fine.
Spring
Follicular · the build-up
What is happening
  • Oestrogen climbing fast.
  • Energy, mood and motivation tend to rise with it.
  • "I can handle this. I feel optimistic."
Spring
Working with it
Social battery
How to work with it
  • School: many find hard work feels easier, a good week to start essays and tackle tricky topics.
  • Movement: rising energy suits harder training or a new sport.
  • Social: 80% and rising. Up for new things and speaking up.
Summer
Ovulation · the peak
What is happening
  • Oestrogen peaks; brief testosterone spike.
  • Confidence is often highest; words can feel like they flow.
  • "I feel good and ready to be seen."
Summer
Working with it
Social battery
How to work with it
  • School: if you can choose, schedule presentations, debates and interviews now.
  • Movement: strong for competition and PBs. Always warm up, injury risk may shift across the cycle.
  • Social: full charge, with social magnetism and persuasiveness.
Autumn
Luteal · the run-up
What is happening
  • Oestrogen drops, progesterone rises then falls.
  • Serotonin and dopamine dip; the brain hunts for flaws.
  • The Inner Critic arrives. Patience runs thin.
Autumn
Working with it
Social battery
How to work with it
  • School: that critical eye is great for editing, proofreading and finishing.
  • Movement: maintenance, not gains. Energy may dip, so keep it steady and don't judge this week's numbers.
  • Social: guard your patience. Expect friendship friction.
Perception vs Reality

The Mirror Lie

  • "Nobody likes me." "I'll fail everything." "I look terrible."
  • These are a chemical side-effect, not facts.
"My brain is low on oestrogen today. This is a side-effect, not the truth. I'll look again in five days."
Movement & sport across the month

Train with your body, not against it

  • You can train on your period, it often helps cramps.
  • Energy rises and falls; an off week is not lost fitness.
  • Heavy periods can lower iron and leave you tired, worth checking.
  • Warm up properly every time; injury risk may shift across the cycle.
  • Top athletes track their cycle to plan training, not to make excuses.
When it is more than a tough week

Ask for help if…

  • Pain that stops your normal life, or very heavy bleeding.
  • Periods gone for months when you're not expecting it.
  • Severe mood crashes, which could be PMDD, which is treatable.
  • Endometriosis and PCOS are common and treatable too.
Common does not mean you have to put up with it.
For the girls
"I'm in a low-energy part of my cycle, so I'm taking it easy tonight."
  • A reason, not an apology.
For the boys
  • If she's suddenly off, it's probably not about you.
  • Never "you're just being hormonal."
  • Give space or support. Don't decide what she can handle.
How to track your cycle

Stop being ambushed by a week you saw coming

  • Mark day 1 (first day of bleeding) and note how you feel.
  • An app, a notes page or a paper calendar all work.
  • After 2–3 months you'll see your own pattern.
The whole point

Your cycle is information.

Girls: know your season, and work with your body, not against it.
Boys: you understand it now, so you can support her, not dismiss her.
