Step 1 · Find Day 1

Day 1 is the first day of proper bleeding (not light spotting). Every cycle is counted from there, up to the day before your next period starts. That total is your cycle length.

Step 2 · Find your seasons

Your cycle length changes where each season falls. Autumn (the run-up to your period) stays roughly two weeks; Spring stretches or shrinks with your length. Use the closest column, or work out your own.

Work out yours

  1. Count your cycle length: Day 1 to the day before your next period.
  2. Your Summer (ovulation) is roughly your length minus 14.
  3. Autumn is the days after that, up to your next period.
  4. Winter is your period; Spring is the gap in between.

Remember: these are estimates. When you first start menstruating, cycles are often irregular and can change month to month, so do not worry if yours does not match the chart. The whole point of tracking is to discover your real pattern, which only you can see.
